    📜 The script — the ODE system and Monod's law
    dS/dt= −1YXS· μ(S) ·X (sugar eaten while bugs grow)
    dX/dt= μ(S) ·X (growth = rate × crowd)
    dP/dt=1 − YXSYXS· μ(S) ·X (the sugar fraction not built into bugs becomes product)
    μ(S)= μmax ·SKs + S Monod (1942): food-limited speed of growth

    Monod's law is the speed limit of the whole story. When S ≫ Ks the fraction S/(Ks+S) ≈ 1, so μ ≈ μmax: full throttle. When S = Ks the cells run at exactly half speed. When S ≪ Ks, μ shrinks almost in proportion to S — famine mode. It is a hyperbola, like Michaelis–Menten, and it is the nonlinear heartbeat inside these three ODEs.

    A hidden invariant glues the cast together: dividing the X-equation by the S-equation gives dX/dS = −Y_XS, so S = S₀ − (X − X₀)/Y_XS. Sugar does not vanish — it becomes bugs (and a bit of product). That is why the final X is exactly X₀ + Y_XS·S₀.

    And because all substrate must end up as biomass or product, the balance closes without a free product-yield parameter: dS/dt = −(dX/dt + dP/dt), i.e. total mass S + X + P = S₀ + X₀ is conserved exactly. Product is simply the leftover sugar mass: dP/dt = (1/Y_XS − 1)·μ(S)·X, so Y_PX = (1 − Y_XS)/Y_XS is derived, not a slider, and P_f = (1 − Y_XS)·S₀. (Watch out for the flipped ratio Y_XS/(1 − Y_XS): it only conserves mass at Y_XS = 0.5.)

    🎭 Meet the cast — who are S, X and P?

    S — Substrate is the food: a sugar like glucose, measured in g/L. It only decreases — microbes eat it, and eating is the engine of everything else.

    X — Biomass is the living crowd: the concentration of cells (g/L). Cells eat sugar and split, so X grows — at a speed set by the menu size through Monod's law.

    P — Product is what we harvest: e.g. ethanol, lactic acid, antibiotics (g/L). Here P is growth-associated and the balance is closed: of every gram of sugar eaten, Y_XS g becomes bugs and the remaining (1 − Y_XS) g becomes product — so Y_PX = (1 − Y_XS)/Y_XS is derived, not a free slider.

    🧮 Behind the camera — how the system is solved

    Numerical star: a classical 4th-order Runge–Kutta (RK4) scheme with 2400 fixed steps integrates (S, X, P) from t = 0 to t_f. RK4 samples each slope four times per step (k₁…k₄) and blends them, giving tiny error even with Monod's nonlinearity — smooth enough to look effortless on screen.

    There is also a closed form. Using the yield invariant S = S₀ − (X − X₀)/Y_XS inside the X-equation separates the variables (A = S₀ + X₀/Y_XS):

    t(X) =1μmax[ (1 + Ks/A) · ln(X/X₀)+ (Ks/A) · ln(S₀/S)]
    valid while S > 0 — this implicit formula is what the ✅ check in the results card verifies against the RK4 movie

    Push Ks → 0 and it collapses to the textbook exponential law X = X₀·e^(μmax·t); push S low and growth stalls — the formula reproduces every scene of the cartoon.
